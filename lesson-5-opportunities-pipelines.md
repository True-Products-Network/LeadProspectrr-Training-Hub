# Lesson 5: Opportunities & Pipelines in LeadProspectrr

## Module Title
**Stop Losing Leads: How to Use Opportunities & Pipelines in LeadProspectrr**

---

**Duration:** 45-60 minutes  
**Target Audience:** Coaches, Consultants, Speakers, Trainers, Service business owners  
**Prerequisites:** Basic familiarity with LeadProspectrr interface

---

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1. Explain what an Opportunity is in LeadProspectrr

## Key Concepts

### What is an Opportunity?
An Opportunity is a lead or contact that could become a client or customer.

**Examples:**
- Someone fills out a form
- Someone books a call
- Someone asks for pricing
- Someone replies to a text or email
- Someone asks about working together

### What is a Pipeline?
A Pipeline is the path a lead moves through as they go from first interest to final decision.

### Pipeline Stages
A Pipeline Stage is one step in the sales process.

**Standard Stages:**
1. **New Lead** - Interest shown, no action taken yet
2. **Contacted** - Business has replied or reached out
3. **Appointment Booked** - Call or consultation scheduled
4. **Proposal Sent** - Pricing/proposal delivered
5. **Won** - Lead agreed to move forward
6. **Lost** - Lead not moving forward at this time

### Follow-Up
Follow-up is the next action needed to keep the conversation moving.

## Lesson Structure

### Section 1: The Problem (5 minutes)

**The Big Idea:**
Most leads are not lost because the person said no. They are lost because:
- The business owner did not follow up at the right time
- They did not know where the lead stood
- They forgot the next step

**Key Distinction:**
> The inbox shows the conversation. The pipeline shows the business status.

**Goal of this lesson:** Help users clearly see who needs attention next—not create a complex sales system.

---

### Section 2: Understanding Opportunities (7 minutes)

**Definition:** An Opportunity is a person or business that could become a client.

**Examples by Business Type:**

| Business Type | Opportunity Example |
|--------------|---------------------|
| Coach | Someone downloads a leadership guide and asks about coaching |
| Consultant | Business owner asks for help fixing a messy sales process |
| Speaker | Event planner asks about availability and speaking fees |
| Service Provider | Potential client fills out a form asking for website/CRM help |

**Key Point:** These people should not just sit in the inbox. They should be tracked as Opportunities.

---

### Section 3: Understanding Pipelines (7 minutes)

**Definition:** A Pipeline is a visual board that shows where each lead stands.

**Simple Analogy:**
> A pipeline is like a row of buckets. Each lead sits in the bucket that matches where they are right now.

**Visual Pipeline:**
```
[New Lead] → [Contacted] → [Appointment Booked] → [Proposal Sent] → [Won/Lost]
```

**Benefits:**
- Know who needs a reply
- Know who needs a proposal
- Know who may be ready to buy

---

### Section 4: Navigating the Opportunities Area (8 minutes)

**Demonstration Points:**

1. **Location:** Where to find Opportunities in LeadProspectrr
2. **Board Layout:** How stages appear visually
3. **Opportunity Cards:** Each card represents a possible customer
4. **Card Details:**
   - Contact information
   - Notes section
   - Opportunity value
   - Current stage
   - Next steps

**Trainer Note:** Keep this section slow. Goal is user confidence, not speed.

---

### Section 5: Creating a Sample Opportunity (8 minutes)

**Example Lead:**
- **Name:** Sarah Johnson
- **Interest:** Website redesign
- **Estimated Value:** $2,500
- **Current Stage:** New Lead
- **Next Step:** Book a call

**Step-by-Step Process:**

1. Click "Add Opportunity"
2. Select the correct Pipeline
3. Place lead in correct stage (New Lead)
4. Add estimated value ($2,500)
5. Add short note: "Interested in website redesign, needs consultation"
6. Save the Opportunity

**Important:** The value doesn't have to be perfect—it's there to help understand possible revenue.

---

### Section 6: Moving Leads Through the Pipeline (8 minutes)

**Continuing with Sarah Johnson example:**

| Stage | What It Means |
|-------|---------------|
| New Lead | Interest shown, no action taken |
| Contacted | Business has replied/reached out |
| Appointment Booked | Call/consultation scheduled |
| Proposal Sent | Pricing/proposal delivered |
| Won | Lead agreed to move forward |
| Lost | Not moving forward at this time |

**Key Point:** "Lost" is not a failure—it simply means the lead is no longer active in this pipeline.

**Demonstration:** Show how to drag/move cards between stages and update notes at each step.

---

### Section 7: Daily Follow-Up Routine (7 minutes)

**The 5-Minute Daily Check:**

1. Open Opportunities
2. Check New Leads
3. Check Contacted leads
4. Check Appointment Booked
5. Check Proposal Sent
6. Move anything that has changed
7. Add notes where needed
8. Set the next follow-up action

**Golden Rule:**
> A pipeline only works if it becomes part of the daily habit.

---

### Section 8: Common Mistakes to Avoid (5 minutes)

**Pipeline Killers:**

- ❌ Creating too many stages
- ❌ Not moving leads after a conversation
- ❌ Leaving old leads in active stages
- ❌ Forgetting to add notes
- ❌ Using the inbox as the sales tracker
- ❌ Not checking the pipeline regularly
- ❌ Treating the pipeline like admin work instead of money tracking

**Simple Coaching Point:**
> Every lead should have a clear stage and a clear next step.

## Real-World Scenario: The Forgotten Lead

**Scenario:**
> A business owner receives this message: "Hi, I saw your post about helping speakers follow up after events. I would like to know more about how that works."
>
> The owner replies once, then gets busy and forgets to follow up.

**Discussion Questions:**

1. **Should this person be added as an Opportunity?**
   - ✅ **Answer:** Yes

2. **What stage should they start in?**
   - **Answer:** New Lead or Contacted (depending on whether you've replied)

3. **What note should be added?**
   - **Example:** "Interested in post-event follow-up system for speakers. Needs details and possible consultation."

4. **What is the next action?**
   - **Example:** "Send short reply and invite them to book a call"

## Module Summary

Opportunities and Pipelines help users see the status of each possible client. Instead of trying to remember who needs follow-up, users can place leads into clear stages and move them as the sales conversation changes.

**The System Works Best When Simple:**
1. Add the lead
2. Choose the stage
3. Add a note
4. Set the next action
5. Check the pipeline daily

**Bottom Line:** A clean pipeline helps users protect their leads, improve follow-up, and turn more conversations into clients.
